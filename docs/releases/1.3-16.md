# Departs 1.3 (16) TestFlight

Departs Now 1.3 (16) gets the walking route to a stop from Apple MapKit on the device. Builds through 1.3 (15) asked the Departs backend, which called Mapbox. Stops and departures still come from the backend at `https://api.departs.now`. The marketing version stays 1.3 and the build number advances to 16.

## What changes since 1.3 (15)

All of it comes from [PR #63](https://github.com/p3ob7o/Departs/pull/63).

- The app requests one walking route per stop from MapKit and uses its line, travel time and distance on the home list and the line screen. It no longer calls `/api/directions`.
- Departures never wait for a walking route. The 30-second departure refresh keeps its pace while routes are loading, and a route that fails leaves departures as they are.
- A walking request gives up after 12 seconds. Home and the line screen share three requests at a time, and identical requests share one calculation.
- A request is cancelled when the rider moves more than 40 metres, when its stop is no longer shown, and when the last window becomes inactive. A cancelled or late result is never shown.
- The store reuses a walk for ten minutes within 40 metres of its origin. An exact origin and destination is reused for one hour in memory, for at most 32 routes.
- With location access denied or restricted the app requests no walking route, shows none and keeps none for reuse.

The backend is unchanged. It keeps `/api/directions` and its Mapbox binding because 1.3 (15) still calls them.

## What to test

Open Departs Now near stops and check that each row shows a walking time. Open a line and check the walking time and the route line on its map. Walk a little and pull to refresh: the walking time should follow you. Send the app to the background, return, and check that the walking time comes back.

## After this build

The backend still serves `/api/directions` and holds its Mapbox binding for 1.3 (15). Removing both is a separate change, to be made once 1.3 (16) has replaced 1.3 (15) on the testers' devices.

## Unverified

- The build on a physical iPhone or iPad. Every check above ran on a simulator.
- How often iOS throttles MapKit directions on a device. The app treats a throttled request as a failed walk and keeps departures visible.
- Before the location prompt is answered, the app can request a walk from a saved area, as earlier builds did. With this build that request goes to Apple, where earlier builds sent it to the Departs backend.
